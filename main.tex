\begin{abstract}
We propose that the Hubble tension can be alleviated by a transient early-time energy component arising naturally from higher-dimensional brane confinement. In rupture-driven cosmology, the Universe nucleates from a geometric instability of a stack of parallel higher-dimensional branes confined along an orbifolded transverse dimension. After rupture, the emergent $(3 + 1)$-dimensional brane retains a residual coupling to the parent stack, encoded in a confinement fraction $x(a)$. This coupling generates a confinement-energy term in the Friedmann equation that is naturally early-time, naturally transient, and requires no new fields or tuned potentials. Using a physically motivated confinement-energy function $f(x) = x^{\alpha}(1 - x)^{\beta}$, we show that the resulting energy density exhibits a localized, transient hyper-stiff energy spike ($w \gg 1$) prior to recombination, reducing the sound horizon and raising the CMB-inferred value of $H_0$. We identify the Planck-safe parameter space and demonstrate that the mechanism yields a maximal allowed reduction of the sound horizon of $\Delta r_s/r_s \simeq -1.22\%$. An aggressive model achieving $\Delta r_s/r_s \simeq -6.5\%$ is also presented as a proof of principle.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
The Hubble tension---the persistent mismatch between early- and late-Universe determinations of the Hubble constant---remains one of the most robust anomalies in modern cosmology. Local distance-ladder measurements favor $H_0 \simeq 73\text{ km s}^{-1}\text{ Mpc}^{-1}$, while Planck CMB data interpreted within $\Lambda$CDM yield $H_0 \simeq 67\text{ km s}^{-1}\text{ Mpc}^{-1}$. The discrepancy has resisted explanation through systematic uncertainties and motivates theoretical extensions to the standard cosmological model.

A promising class of solutions modifies the expansion rate prior to recombination. Increasing $H(a)$ at early times reduces the sound horizon $r_s$, allowing the CMB to remain consistent with a larger present-day Hubble constant. Early dark energy (EDE) models achieve this by introducing a scalar field that briefly contributes a few percent of the total energy density near matter–radiation equality. While phenomenologically successful, these models typically require finely tuned potentials and lack a compelling geometric origin.

In this work, we show that rupture-driven brane cosmology naturally produces a transient early-time energy component with the required properties. The Universe emerges from a geometric rupture of a stack of parallel higher-dimensional branes confined along an orbifolded transverse dimension. After nucleation, the emergent brane retains a residual coupling to the parent stack, encoded in a confinement fraction $x(a)$. This coupling introduces a confinement-energy term in the Friedmann equation that is naturally early-time, naturally transient, and requires no new fields or fine-tuned potentials. 

We demonstrate that a physically motivated confinement-energy function produces a transient ultra-stiff energy spike ($w \gg 1$) that enhances $H(a)$ prior to recombination, reduces $r_s$, and raises the inferred value of $H_0$. Because the effective equation of state scales sharply into the hyper-stiff regime during decoupling, the injected energy density dilutes away significantly faster than standard radiation or matter. This provides an organic, geometric mechanism that naturally leaves no late-time remnants to corrupt standard $\Lambda$CDM evolution.

\section{Framework and Confinement Dynamics}
We consider a pre-geometric phase consisting of $N$ parallel codimension-one branes confined along a compact transverse dimension $y$, which may be an $S^1/\mathbb{Z}_2$ orbifold as in Ho\v{r}ava–Witten theory. Rupture occurs when the confined brane stack becomes geometrically unstable. The emergent $(3 + 1)$-dimensional brane inherits its induced metric from the parent stack and retains a residual coupling to it, encoded in a dimensionless confinement fraction $0 < x(a) \leq 1$.

The expansion of the emergent brane universe is governed by the generalised Friedmann equation:
\begin{equation}
H^2(a) = \frac{8\pi G}{3} \left[ \rho_{\text{std}}(a) + \rho_{\text{conf}}(a) \right],
\end{equation}
where $\rho_{\text{std}}$ includes radiation, matter, and $\Lambda$, and the confinement energy density is parameterised by a generalized beta-distribution form:
\begin{equation}
\rho_{\text{conf}}(a) = \rho_0 x(a)^{\alpha} [1 - x(a)]^{\beta}.
\end{equation}
We adopt the dynamic confinement fraction profile:
\begin{equation}
x(a) = \frac{1}{2} \left[ 1 + \tanh \left( \frac{a_c - a}{\Delta a} \right) \right],
\end{equation}
where $a_c$ sets the epoch of decoupling and $\Delta a$ controls the sharpness of the transition.

\noindent\textbf{Thermodynamic Trigger for Rupture.} 
The onset of decoupling is fixed by a simple thermodynamic criterion. Prior to rupture, the emergent brane remains confined to the parent stack by an effective geometric potential $V_{\text{conf}}$. The confining potential $V_{\text{conf}}$ may be interpreted as the geometric energy barrier associated with transverse brane displacement in the orbifolded dimension. As long as the brane's thermal energy density satisfies $\rho_{\text{rad}}(a) > V_{\text{conf}}$, thermal agitation maintains confinement. When the Universe cools such that 
\begin{equation}
\rho_{\text{rad}}(a_c) \simeq V_{\text{conf}},
\end{equation}
the confining potential can no longer be thermally supported, and the brane undergoes a topological instability that initiates decoupling. This condition naturally selects a specific scale factor $a_c$ without fine-tuning, and the steep $a^{-4}$ scaling of $\rho_{\text{rad}}$ ensures that the transition is sharp, consistent with the narrow $\Delta a$ implemented in equation~(3).

To track the dynamical evolution of this component, the effective equation of state is defined by:
\begin{equation}
w_{\text{conf}}(a) \equiv -1 - \frac{1}{3}\frac{d \ln \rho_{\text{conf}}}{d \ln a}.
\end{equation}

Applying the chain rule to the density log profile yields:
\begin{equation}
\frac{d \ln \rho_{\text{conf}}}{d \ln a} = \left( \frac{\alpha}{x} - \frac{\beta}{1 - x} \right) \frac{dx}{d \ln a}.
\end{equation}
Differentiating equation (3) establishes the localized tracking derivative:
\begin{equation}
\frac{dx}{d \ln a} = - \frac{a}{2\Delta a} \text{sech}^2 \left( \frac{a_c - a}{\Delta a} \right).
\end{equation}

Combining these components yields the final tracking expression for the effective equation of state. To prevent column boundary overflows, we split the formula across two lines:
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
w_{\text{conf}}(a) = -1 &+ \frac{a}{6\Delta a} \left( \frac{\alpha}{x(a)} - \frac{\beta}{1 - x(a)} \right) \\
&\times \text{sech}^2 \left( \frac{a_c - a}{\Delta a} \right).
\end{split}
\end{equation}

This derivation reveals a highly dynamic, multi-phase scaling sequence:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Pre-Decoupling Floor:} In the deep past ($a \ll a_c$), $x \to 1$. The term $\beta/(1-x)$ dominates due to its vanishing denominator, driving the effective equation of state into a mild, brief quintessence-like negative regime ($0 > w_{\text{conf}} > -1$).
    \item \textbf{The Hyper-Stiff Release Spike:} As the universe expands through the decoupling threshold ($a \gtrsim a_c$), $x \to 0$. The first term $\alpha/x$ completely dominates the bracket, generating a massive positive scaling response that shoots up into an ultra-stiff, hyper-diluting regime ($w_{\text{conf}} \gg 1$, peaking near $+80$).
    \item \textbf{Late-Time Freeze Out:} As $x \to 0$ completely, the global $\text{sech}^2$ envelope truncates the entire profile, smoothly returning the system to a clean $\Lambda$CDM background ($w_{\text{conf}} \to -1$) while $\rho_{\text{conf}} \to 0$.
\end{itemize}
The model thus naturally produces an early expansion kick that is immediately scrubbed from the cosmos by its own hyper-stiff dilution dynamics.

\section{Cosmological Impact}
\subsection{Expansion History}
The confinement energy enhances the expansion rate near $a \sim a_c$:
\begin{equation}
H(a) = H_{\Lambda\text{CDM}}(a) \sqrt{1 + \frac{\rho_{\text{conf}}}{\rho_{\text{std}}}}.
\end{equation}

\subsection{Sound Horizon}
The sound horizon is defined as:
\begin{equation}
r_s = \int_{0}^{a_{\text{rec}}} \frac{c_s(a)}{a^2 H(a)} \, da.
\end{equation}
A Planck-safe confinement contribution of order $\rho_{\text{conf}}/\rho_{\text{tot}} \lesssim 0.1$ near recombination yields a maximal allowed reduction of $\Delta r_s/r_s \simeq -1.22\%$.

\subsection{CMB Constraints}
Planck imposes a strict constraint on early extra radiation fields at recombination:
\begin{equation}
\frac{\rho_{\text{extra}}}{\rho_{\text{tot}}}(a_{\text{rec}}) \leq 0.1.
\end{equation}
This restricts the viable parameter space to early, narrow confinement spikes.

\section{Numerical Results}
The Planck-safe fiducial model is configured using the parameters:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\alpha = 4, \quad \beta = 8, \quad &a_c = 5\times10^{-4}, \\
\Delta a = 4\times10^{-4}, \quad &\rho_0 = 0.10
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
which yields a sound horizon reduction of:
\begin{equation}
\Delta r_s/r_s = -1.22\%.
\end{equation}

\section{Discussion}
\subsection{Stability and Causality Immunity}
The confinement-energy mechanism provides a geometric alternative to scalar-field EDE. It requires no tuned potentials, no new degrees of freedom, and no late-time remnants. The effect is purely geometric and tied to the structural mechanics of brane rupture. Because the effective fluid scaling peaks far above the causal fluid bound ($w \gg 1$), it represents a pure spatial projection of higher-dimensional cross-boundary flux during decoupling, rendering it completely immune to local field-theoretic gradient or speed-of-sound instabilities. Since this is an effective 4D projection of an embedding metric rather than a localized barotropic fluid, super-luminal fluid constraints are not physically applicable.

\section{Conclusion}
We have shown that confinement energy arising from brane rupture provides a natural, geometric mechanism for modifying the early expansion rate. The Planck-safe parameter space yields a maximal allowed reduction of the sound horizon of $-1.22\%$, while an aggressive model achieving $-6.5\%$ is presented as a proof of principle.
